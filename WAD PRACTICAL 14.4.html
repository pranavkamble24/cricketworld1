ICC



ICC can refer to the International Criminal Court, a permanent tribunal prosecuting genocide, war crimes, and crimes against humanity, or the International Cricket Council, the global governing body for cricket, regulating the sport and its major tournaments. The Court, based in The Hague, acts as a court of last resort, while the Council, based in Dubai, promotes and develops cricket worldwide. Both are crucial international bodies, but serve very different functions in justice and sports.

International Criminal Court (ICC)
Role: Investigates and prosecutes individuals for the most serious international crimes: genocide, crimes against humanity, war crimes, and aggression.
Function: A court of last resort, stepping in when national courts are unwilling or unable to act.
Founded: Established by the Rome Statute, which entered into force in 2002, according to Drishti IAS and Vision IAS.

International Cricket Council (ICC)
Role: Global governing body for cricket, unifying nations through the sport and overseeing its growth.
Function: Regulates cricket, organizes major events like the Cricket World Cup, and enforces anti-corruption/doping measures.
Founded: Established in 1909 as the Imperial Cricket Conference
