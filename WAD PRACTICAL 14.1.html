Board of Control for Cricket in India (BCCI)



The Board of Control for Cricket in India (BCCI) is India's national governing body for cricket, managing all domestic and international aspects, including team selection, scheduling, and development, operating from Mumbai and serving as the world's wealthiest cricket board. Formed in 1928, it's a private entity funded by broadcasting rights, sponsorships, and gate fees, not government funds, overseeing the Indian Men's and Women's national teams and running the National Cricket Academy (NCA). The BCCI also spearheads major leagues like the IPL and WPL, significantly influencing global cricket administration.

Role: Governs all cricket in India, selects players, coaches, and manages fixtures.
Structure: A consortium of state associations, a private body, and a major global powerhouse.
Funding: Self-funded through commercial rights, not government grants.
Key Initiatives: Runs the National Cricket Academy (NCA) and organizes the Indian Premier League (IPL) and Women's Premier League (WPL).
Influence: Known as the richest board, a key player in world cricket governance.
